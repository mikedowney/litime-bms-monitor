Apogee — BMS Monitor

Bluetooth telemetry from four LiTime LiFePO4 house batteries, collected on the Cerbo GX, stored locally, and served as a dashboard and API.

← Back to the dashboard

Overview

The four house batteries have Bluetooth BMS modules that expose far more detail than the LiTime phone app shows. The app reports only "balance OK, cells OK, BMS OK". The BMS itself reports individual cell voltages, two temperatures, per-pack current, capacity, cycle count, balancing state and protection flags.

This system polls all four packs over Bluetooth Low Energy from the Cerbo GX, stores every reading in a local SQLite database, and presents it through a web dashboard and a JSON API. The API also feeds the automated daily AI analysis that runs in Node-RED.

What this is not. It registers nothing on the Victron dbus. It does not create a battery service, cannot be selected by DVCC, and cannot influence charging in any way. It is read-only monitoring. This is deliberate: an earlier attempt using dbus-serialbattery did register as a controlling BMS, which caused the solar chargers to latch into Err 67 — No BMS and stop charging when the Bluetooth connection dropped.

Why it matters

The primary purpose is detecting when a BMS drops offline. Four packs in parallel are each rated 200 A continuous. With all four online a 236 A load is 59 A per pack. With two offline it is 118 A each — still within rating. You do not exceed a pack's limit until three are offline. But without this system you would have no way of knowing how many are participating, because the SmartShunt sees only the bank total.

Collection

A single background thread polls the packs in round-robin order. For each pack it connects over BLE, writes an eight-byte status request, reassembles the 105-byte reply from MTU-sized notification fragments, verifies the checksum, decodes it, and writes one row to samples. Every attempt — success or failure — also writes a row to polls, which is what makes the reliability statistics possible.

Packs are polled sequentially, never in parallel. Each BMS accepts only one BLE client at a time, and the adapter handles one connection setup more reliably than four at once. Between packs the poller waits --settle seconds (default 12) so BlueZ can finish tearing the previous link down.

Persistent discovery

Venus OS has no persistent BlueZ device storage — /var/lib/bluetooth/<adapter>/ does not exist. Nothing is bonded, trust is not saved across restarts, and the device cache lives only in memory. BlueZ prunes devices it has not seen recently.

Consequence: packs silently vanish from the D-Bus object tree and become unreachable, which presented as two of four packs failing every cycle. The collector therefore keeps one discovery session open permanently and re-checks it each cycle. This is not a workaround for a transient fault; it is the structural fix for how this platform behaves.

Storage and retention

SQLite in WAL mode. Raw samples are kept for --prune-days (30 by default), then folded into a daily rollup table holding min/max and alarm state per pack per day, and deleted. Roughly 530 bytes per sample; at a 60-second interval that is about 2 MB/day and 60 MB for a 30-day window.

Derived metrics

Two figures are computed rather than read from the BMS:

  • SoC drift — the BMS's reported state of charge compared against its own remaining ÷ capacity arithmetic. Exposes a counter that has overrun; one pack has been observed at 114%.
  • Internal resistance — the difference between the sum of cell voltages and the terminal voltage, fitted against current. The slope is the resistance of the FETs, shunt, busbars and welds. See Thresholds.

FET state (bytes 68–71)

BitMeaningConfidence
0x80Discharge disabled. The pack is electrically isolated and carries none of the load. Set by the discharge switch in the LiTime app; persists until switched back on. Confirmed 2026-09-15 by toggling the app switch with the collector stopped and observing the flag change in both directions. Matches the reference implementation's documented 0x00000080.
0x48Not accepting charge — the pack considers itself full and has opened its charge FET. Normal. Every healthy pack sets this at the top of charge; it appears in roughly a fifth of all logged samples. Inferred from correlation with full charge across all four packs. Which of 0x08 and 0x40 does what individually is unknown; they have only ever been observed together.

A pack with discharge disabled reports no protection bit, no failure bit, and perfectly healthy cells. Nothing in the alarm fields shows it. Battery 4 ran isolated from 2026-09-13 to 2026-09-15 and was only found because the terminal voltage tracked the bank while its cell voltages stayed frozen, and because three packs accounted for the entire SmartShunt current during a 100 A load test.

Protection bits (bytes 76–79)

0x00000004  over charge          0x00000200  high temperature 2
0x00000020  over discharge       0x00000400  low temperature 1
0x00000040  charge over current  0x00000800  low temperature 2
0x00000080  discharge over cur.  0x00004000  short circuit
0x00000100  high temperature 1

These bit definitions are inherited from another battery model and have never been validated on this hardware, because no protection event has yet occurred. The field decodes correctly and reads zero on healthy packs, but which bit means what is unproven. Treat any non-zero value as "something tripped" and investigate directly rather than trusting the label.

Database schema

samples

One row per successful poll. 39 columns.

GroupColumns
Identityts, mac, label
Measurementsvoltage, cells_sum, v_drop, current, power, temp_cell, temp_mosfet
Cellscells, cells_all, cell_count, cell_min, cell_max, cell_delta_mv
Capacityremaining_ah, capacity, soc, soc_computed, soc_drift, soh, cycles, total_discharged_ah
Flagsstate, state_raw, protections, protection_raw, failure_raw, balancing, balancing_raw, balance_memory_raw, flags68_raw, full_charge, balance_latch
Reservedreserved56_raw, reserved60_raw, frame_len
Rawraw — full frame hex

Flags are stored as integers, not hex strings, so bitwise SQL works directly: WHERE protection_raw & 4 finds over-charge events.

The raw column is deliberate insurance. If any byte-offset interpretation later proves wrong, every sample can be re-decoded from the database without re-collecting anything.

polls

ts, mac, ok, error. One row per attempt. This is what distinguishes "the pack is quiet" from "the pack did not answer".

daily

Keyed on (day, mac). Populated automatically as raw samples age out, so long-term trends survive pruning.

HTTP API

Bound per --bind in the run script. Read-only; there are no write endpoints and no authentication.

Every windowed endpoint accepts either form. ?hours=N for a rolling window, or ?from=&to= for an explicit range. Accepted date formats are YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS, YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM, YYYY-MM-DD, and the T-separated form a browser sends. Unparseable input falls back to the default window rather than erroring.

/bms/summary?from=2026-09-11 18:00&to=2026-09-12 02:00
/bms/history?from=2026-09-11T18:00&to=2026-09-11T22:00

/bms/summary returns window_is_live and window_end so a caller can tell a historical window from a current one. Staleness is judged against the end of the window, not the clock - otherwise every pack looks stale on a historical query.

Controls

  • Click any pack name in a legend to hide or show it across every chart at once. Useful when four near-identical traces overlap.
  • Time range presets from 1 hour to 7 days, plus Custom range… which reveals two datetime pickers. Only the charts follow the selected window; cards and banner stay live.
  • Charts spanning more than a day draw a dashed rule at each local midnight with the date, and switch to date-aware axis labels.
  • Auto-refreshes every 60 seconds.

Terminal voltages are not comparable between packs. The packs are hard-paralleled, so the true voltage is identical for all four; the 50–150 mV spread you see is each BMS's own pack-voltage calibration offset.

Measured on this bank, the offsets are stable to about 6 mV each: Battery 1 +99, Battery 2 +37, Battery 3 +53, Battery 4 +49 mV. Subtracting them collapses the pack-to-pack disagreement at rest from 63 mV to 14 mV — the same agreement the cell sums achieve with no correction at all.

Compare cell sums between packs instead. Three independent BMS have been observed agreeing within 1 mV and matching the SmartShunt, which is strong evidence the cell measurement chain is accurate and the pack-voltage channel is the one carrying the error.

Query tool

bmsq exists because installing the sqlite3 command-line package would consume scarce space on the root filesystem. It uses Python's built-in sqlite3 module and sets the boat timezone for itself.

./bmsq                          default report
./bmsq --latest                 newest reading per pack
./bmsq --reliability 24         poll success plus recent failures
./bmsq --alarms 168             any alarm or balancing activity
./bmsq --tables                 schema and row counts
./bmsq --csv "SELECT ..."       CSV output for piping
./bmsq "SELECT ..."             arbitrary SQL

Useful queries

# Current sharing during real load
./bmsq "SELECT label, COUNT(*) n, ROUND(AVG(current),2) avg_a
        FROM samples WHERE current < -10 GROUP BY label"

# Is a pack's FET open? v_drop inverts when cells are isolated
./bmsq "SELECT label, ROUND(AVG(v_drop),3) avg_vdrop,
        SUM(CASE WHEN current=0 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) zero_current
        FROM samples GROUP BY label"

# Capacity trend from the rollup table
./bmsq "SELECT day, mac, cycles, delta_max FROM daily ORDER BY day DESC LIMIT 20"

drop is a reserved word in SQLite. Alias v_drop as something else, or quote it.

Troubleshooting

Start at /logs. The last few hundred log lines, with failures in red and discovery or prune events in amber, refreshing every 30 seconds. Readable from a phone at the helm without an SSH session. It shows the current log file only — roughly a day and a half at the normal poll rate. The database is the long-term record.

Dashboard unreachable

svstat /service/bms-collector
netstat -tln | grep 8088
grep bind /data/bms/service/run

If the process is up but the port is not listening, --bind is probably 127.0.0.1. Historically this was caused by re-running an older installer that overwrote the setting; the current installer preserves it.

A pack stops reporting

./bmsq "SELECT mac, error, COUNT(*) n FROM polls
        WHERE ok=0 AND ts > strftime('%s','now')-14400 GROUP BY mac, error"

Reset the Bluetooth adapter

svc -d /service/bms-collector
hciconfig hci0 down && hciconfig hci0 up
sleep 3
bluetoothctl --timeout 20 scan on | grep C8:47:80
svc -u /service/bms-collector

Check the phone first. Each BMS accepts one Bluetooth client at a time. If the LiTime app is open and connected to a pack, the collector cannot reach it and will log repeated failures for that pack only.

Data looks wrong

Cross-check against Victron. Pack voltages should agree with the SmartShunt within a few tens of millivolts, and the sum of the four pack currents should approximate the SmartShunt bank current. A persistent shortfall means a pack is not participating.

If a decoded field looks implausible, the raw frame is stored and can be re-examined:

./bmsq "SELECT datetime(ts,'unixepoch','localtime'), label, raw
        FROM samples ORDER BY ts DESC LIMIT 4"

Database growth

du -h /data/bms/bms.db*
df -h /data
./bmsq "SELECT COUNT(*) FROM samples"

Expect roughly 2 MB/day at a 60-second interval. Pruning runs once every 24 hours and on startup. If space becomes tight, reduce --prune-days or lengthen --interval.

Alerting

Two independent paths, deliberately separated by what each is good at.

MethodExplicit thresholds in a function nodeGemini, given the data and instructions
Can it miss something?Only if a rule is absentYes — an LLM may vary between runs
Can it invent something?NoYes — it has done, see below

Why the split. The AI is good at "Battery 1's resistance has crept up over three weeks", which is hard to express as a threshold. It is not the right tool for "a pack is offline right now", because it runs once a day, may phrase things differently each time, and cannot be relied on to notice its own input is missing. The deterministic check runs often, always fires, and treats the collector being unreachable as an alert in itself — which is the failure mode that has actually occurred here.

The alert flow

inject (15 min)  →  http request /bms/summary?hours=1
                 →  Evaluate Conditions  (all thresholds live here)
                 →  Format Email
                 →  e-mail

Plus two manual injects: Test now to run a cycle immediately, and Clear alert state to forget what has already been notified after changing a threshold.

A charge FET opening is not an alert on its own. Every healthy pack opens it on reaching full, and it appears in roughly a fifth of all logged samples. Only refusing charge while charge is available, or staying open for days, is flagged. Getting this wrong would mean an email every night the bank fills, and alert fatigue is the real failure mode.

Notification behaviour

  • A new condition emails immediately.
  • An unresolved CRITICAL re-notifies every 12 hours; a WARNING every 24.
  • A condition that clears sends one all-clear.
  • Subject leads with the worst active item, readable on a phone lock screen.
  • Body includes current state for all four packs with FET status, plus links to the dashboard and these docs.

Dedupe state lives in Node-RED flow context, which does not survive a restart. After restarting Node-RED you may get one repeat notification for a still-active condition. That is the deliberate trade: a duplicate is better than a missed alert.

Thresholds are duplicated. They live in the Node-RED function node and in the collector's summary logic. If you change the collector's baselines, update the flow to match. This was a conscious choice — evaluation in Node-RED keeps the alerting readable and editable without touching the collector — but it is the one place the system can drift out of agreement with itself.

Setting it up

  1. Node-RED menu → Import → paste bms-alerts-flow.json → Import to new flow. Node IDs were chosen not to collide with the existing 190 nodes.
  2. Open the e-mail node and enter the Gmail app password. Credentials are never exported, so this is the one manual step.
  3. Deploy, then press Test now. A healthy bank produces nothing, which is correct but uninformative.
  4. To prove the path end to end, temporarily change a threshold in the eval function (for example > 3.65 to > 3.40), deploy, test, confirm the email arrives, then revert and press Clear alert state.

Notes on the daily AI report

Two prompt rules exist because of false positives it produced:

  • Cumulative counters. VE.Bus network error counts and similar only ever increase and reset on power cycle. A value of 2 is background noise. The report once inferred a failing RJ45 cable from it. These must be reported as context, never as an anomaly, unless large or demonstrably rising.
  • Historical alarm suppression. Alarms dated 2026-09-08 to 2026-09-15 relating to the LiTime packs came from a BMS driver that was installed and removed. That rule matches on timestamped records only — it cannot cover an undated counter, and it must not suppress current BMS telemetry.

Thresholds & interpretation

Cell spread

Limits depend on where the bank sits on the charge curve, because LiFePO4 is electrically flat through the middle of its range.

ConditionWatchInvestigate
Plateau, below 3.45 V/cell30 mV60 mV
Top of charge, above 3.45 V/cell50 mV100 mV
Under load, above 20 A80 mV120 mV

Measured baseline on this bank: 14–21 mV at rest, 35–50 mV under load, worst ever recorded 50 mV during a 155 A discharge. Above roughly 160 mV a pack is conventionally considered imbalanced.

Temperature

  • Below 0 °C while charging causes lithium plating — permanent damage and a genuine safety issue. The BMS should block it; this system is how you verify that it did.
  • Above 45 °C ageing accelerates. Above 55 °C the BMS should be limiting current.
  • Pack-to-pack deviation above 5 °C warrants investigation — identical packs sharing a compartment should track within a couple of degrees.
  • MOSFET temperature normally runs a few degrees above cell temperature under load. A gap widening over months at the same current suggests degradation.

Current

  • Deadband is ±2.07 A. Verified: across 3,179 samples the smallest non-zero magnitude was 2.072 A, with nothing whatsoever between 0 and that. A reading of 0.0 A means "under 2 A", never "disconnected".
  • Healthy sharing is within about 3%. Measured baseline: 0.6 A spread across four packs at ±25 A, under 3%.
  • Sustained deviation beyond 10% suggests elevated resistance in a cable, terminal or fuse.

Internal resistance

Fitted from v_drop = offset + I × R using only samples above 10 A. Expect 0.2–0.4 mΩ per pack. The absolute value matters far less than movement over months. Low R² is expected — the calibration offset is noisier than the signal — and does not indicate a fault.

This metric only accumulates when the bank is actually used. At the dock everything sits inside the deadband and no fit is possible.

Known quirks

State of charge disagreement is normal

The four packs are hard-paralleled, so their true state of charge is identical by definition. Reported values have been observed at 99%, 96%, 87% and 46% simultaneously. This is four independent coulomb counters drifting, not a fault. The cause is the 2.07 A deadband: at a dock, refrigeration draws roughly 1.5 A per pack and goes entirely uncounted, accumulating error in one direction.

All four counters have been observed resetting to 100% simultaneously when absorption reached the top. The SmartShunt is authoritative for bank state of charge.

A pack reading 0.0 A at full charge

Normal end-of-charge behaviour. The pack that fills first opens its charge FET and stops accepting, while the others continue. Confirmed by cross-check: three packs summing to 100.18 A matched the SmartShunt's 98.4 A exactly, with the fourth contributing nothing.

When a FET is open, v_drop inverts — terminals sit at bus voltage while the cells float free at their true resting voltage. That sign flip is a reliable indicator, and it is why resistance fitting excludes low-current samples.

Per-pack voltage offsets

Pack terminal voltages disagree by up to 150 mV between packs sampled in the same cycle, despite being hard-paralleled and therefore at an identical true voltage.

The cell measurement chain is the accurate one. Three independent BMS have been observed reporting cell sums within 1 mV of each other and of the SmartShunt. The pack-voltage channel is where the error lives — plausibly because the BMS protects on cell voltages, so that path gets the calibration attention while pack voltage is largely a display number.

Measured offsets, stable to about 6 mV each: Battery 1 +99, Battery 2 +37, Battery 3 +53, Battery 4 +49 mV. Applying them collapses the at-rest disagreement from 63 mV to 14 mV. Under load about 56 mV survives correction, which is real — different cable runs to the busbar.

Compare cell sums between packs, never terminal voltages. The dashboard shows the cell sum and its offset from the terminals on each pack card for exactly this reason; a negative offset means the cells are isolated behind an open FET.

Timezone

Venus OS runs on UTC by design and VRM applies your display timezone. The system clock is deliberately left alone; the collector and bmsq set TZ for their own processes only.

Node-RED inherits the system timezone, so a cron entry of 00 06 * * * fires at 06:00 UTC — 11 PM Pacific the previous evening. Check any scheduled flows.

A pack can latch into an isolated state

Battery 4 sat with both FETs open from 2026-09-13 to 2026-09-15: cells frozen at 3.432 V while its terminals tracked the bank, remaining capacity pinned at exactly 336.00 of 336.00 Ah, contributing nothing to a 100 A load. It reported no protection bit and no failure bit, and the app showed cells, balance and BMS all healthy.

Toggling the app's discharge switch did not clear it. What did: disconnecting the pack from the bank, Power off in the app, a few minutes dark, then waking it with a charger. It has behaved normally since.

The state was therefore held in volatile memory, not flash — which is why only a full controller shutdown cleared it. If this recurs, that is the procedure.

Serial numbers are not retrievable

The serial-number command returns twenty bytes of 0xFF on all four packs. The app's serials come from the label or LiTime's cloud, not from the BMS. Pack identity therefore depends on the mapping table in Hardware — keep it accurate.

Wider system notes

Why absorption is 13.9 V

The packs are rated for 14.4 ±0.2 V, so 13.9 V undercharges them deliberately. The reason is the Orion XS engine-detection logic.

The Orions decide an engine is running by watching their input voltage, with delayed start at 14.00 V and shutdown at 13.50 V. The starter batteries are fed from the MultiPlus trickle output. Measured relationship:

V_starter ≈ V_house + (I × 0.004) − 0.245

The 245 mV is the trickle diode drop; the 4 mΩ is cable resistance between the MultiPlus terminals and the bank. Under heavy charge the cable drop exceeds the diode drop and the starter battery ends up above the house bank. At 14.2 V absorption the starter crosses 14.00 V and both Orions start, cycle, and flap — which is exactly what was observed.

At 13.9 V the ranges do not overlap and the problem disappears. The consequences are that cells never reach the 3.50 V balancing threshold, and the SoC counters never get a full-charge reset.

Periodic remedy: raise absorption to 14.2 V for a couple of hours roughly once a quarter, while aboard and watching. This lets balancing run and recalibrates all four SoC counters. Expect the Orions to cycle during it.

Permanent fix: drive the Orions' remote on/off terminals from a real engine-running signal (alternator D+ or an oil pressure switch) and disable voltage-based detection. Voltage detection cannot work reliably here because a loaded alternator produces 13.5–13.6 V at the Orion input — below its own start threshold and only ~40 mV above its shutdown threshold.

Do not install dbus-serialbattery

It works, and it reads these packs correctly. But it registers as a controlling BMS, which caused all three MPPTs to latch Err 67 — No BMS and stop charging whenever the BLE connection dropped. Clearing that flag requires connecting to each MPPT individually with VictronConnect; it cannot be reset from the Cerbo. Set Controlling BMS to No BMS control and Battery Monitor to the Apogee SmartShunt, both pinned explicitly rather than Automatic.
